Regional introduction

Welcome to North Africa.

North Africa includes Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya and Egypt. Its food has Amazigh (Berber) roots, mixed over centuries with Arab, Andalusian, Ottoman, French and sub-Saharan influences.

In 2020, the knowledge and traditions around couscous were added to UNESCO's list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, after a joint application by Algeria, Mauritania, Morocco and Tunisia.

Food culture

How people eat together.

In many Moroccan and Algerian homes, Friday is couscous day, when the family gathers around one large dish. Sweet mint tea is poured from a height to create foam and is offered to every guest as a sign of welcome.

During Ramadan, the fast is broken at sunset with dates, milk and a warming soup such as Moroccan harira. Bread is treated with great respect, and in some neighbourhoods families still send their dough to a shared bakery oven.

Cooking techniques.

  • Slow braising

    Food cooks gently in a tagine, whose cone-shaped lid catches steam and returns it to the pot.

  • Steaming couscous

    Couscous is steamed in the top part of a couscoussier, above a simmering stew, and fluffed by hand between steamings.

  • Preserving

    Lemons are cured in salt for weeks, and butter is aged into smen to add deep, tangy flavour.

  • Sweet & savoury

    Meat is often cooked with prunes, apricots, honey or cinnamon, as in the famous pigeon or chicken pastilla.
