Regional introduction

Welcome to East Africa.

East Africa includes Ethiopia, Eritrea, Somalia, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda and Burundi. In the highlands, cooks use teff and sorghum; around the Great Lakes, cooking bananas, beans and cassava leaves are everyday foods.

On the coast, centuries of Indian Ocean trade with Arabia, Persia and India brought cloves, cardamom, cinnamon and rice. This created Swahili dishes such as spiced pilau and coconut curries.

Food culture

How people eat together.

In Ethiopia, eating together is a sign of trust. Diners share one large platter of injera, and placing food in a friend's mouth (called gursha) shows love and respect. The Ethiopian coffee ceremony, where beans are roasted, ground and brewed in front of guests, can last more than an hour.

In Rwanda and Burundi, isombe, beans and plantains (ibitoke) are served at family meals, while in Kenya and Tanzania, friends gather around nyama choma (slow-grilled meat) at weekends.

Cooking techniques.

  • Long fermentation

    Teff batter is left to ferment for two to three days, giving injera its sour taste and spongy texture.

  • Spiced butter

    Butter is slowly clarified with garlic, ginger and spices to make niter kibbeh, the flavour base of many Ethiopian stews.

  • Dry-cooking onions

    Ethiopian wots begin by cooking onions without oil until soft and sweet, before any fat or spice is added.

  • Steaming in leaves

    In Uganda, food is wrapped and steamed in banana leaves, as in luwombo, keeping it moist and fragrant.
