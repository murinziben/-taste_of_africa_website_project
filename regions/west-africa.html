Regional introduction

Welcome to West Africa.

West Africa stretches across sixteen countries — including Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Mali, Côte d'Ivoire and Sierra Leone — and its kitchens reflect dry savanna, tropical rainforest and a long Atlantic coastline.

A typical meal pairs a starch with a richly flavoured soup or stew. The starch might be rice or a “swallow”: smooth, stretchy balls of pounded yam, fufu, eba or banku that are pinched with the fingers and dipped into the soup.

Food culture

How people eat together.

Food in West Africa is social. Huge pots of jollof and stew are cooked for weddings, naming ceremonies and festivals, and guests are always offered something to eat. In many homes, family members eat together from a shared bowl, using the right hand.

Street food is just as important: smoky suya skewers in Nigeria, spicy fried plantain (kelewele) in Ghana and bean fritters (akara) sold at dawn. And of course there are the friendly “jollof wars” about which country cooks it best.

Cooking techniques.

  • Pounding

    Boiled yam, cassava or plantain is pounded in a large wooden mortar until smooth and stretchy to make fufu and pounded yam.

  • Building a pepper base

    Tomatoes, peppers and onions are blended and fried in oil until the oil floats to the top — the foundation of jollof and most stews.

  • Fermenting

    Locust beans are fermented into iru or dawadawa, a powerful seasoning, and cassava is fermented and roasted into garri.

  • Smoking & grilling

    Fish is smoked to preserve it and add depth, while suya beef is grilled over open flames with a spicy groundnut rub called yaji.
