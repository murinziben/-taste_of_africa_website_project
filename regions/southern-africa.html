Regional introduction

Welcome to Southern Africa.

Southern Africa includes South Africa, Zimbabwe, Botswana, Namibia, Mozambique, Zambia, Malawi, Lesotho and Eswatini. Across the region, a stiff maize porridge — called pap, sadza or nshima — is the everyday staple.

Colonial history and migration created famous fusion cuisines: Cape Malay cooking in Cape Town, Durban's Indian curries and the peri-peri flavours of Mozambique.

Food culture

How people eat together.

The braai — cooking meat over open coals — is a social ritual in South Africa, and Heritage Day on 24 September is popularly celebrated as “National Braai Day”. In townships, shisa nyama spots let customers pick raw meat and have it grilled on the spot.

Family celebrations often feature umngqusho (samp and beans), slow-cooked stews and chakalaka relish, while bunny chow tells the story of Durban's Indian community.

Cooking techniques.

  • Braai

    Meat, sausages and corn are grilled over wood or charcoal, a slow social ritual rather than a quick meal.

  • Potjiekos

    Meat and vegetables are layered in a three-legged cast-iron pot and cooked for hours over coals without stirring.

  • Drying meat

    Strips of seasoned meat are air-dried into biltong, a preserving method used for centuries.

  • Spice blending

    Cooks blend their own masala for Durban curries and sweeter, aromatic blends for Cape Malay dishes.
