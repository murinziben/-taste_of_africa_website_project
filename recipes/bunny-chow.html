Bunny Chow

A hollowed-out loaf of white bread filled with a fiery Durban mutton and potato curry.

  • Cook1 hr 10
  • Prep15 min
  • Serves4
  • LevelEasy
Start cooking
What you need

Ingredients

Change the servings and every quantity updates automatically. Original recipe serves 4.

For the curry

  • 800 g mutton or lamb, cubed (or chicken thighs)
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 4 cardamom pods, lightly crushed
  • 8 curry leaves
  • 2 medium onions, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 3 tbsp Durban masala (hot curry powder)
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 3 medium tomatoes, grated
  • 2 medium potatoes, cubed
  • 1½ cups water
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • Salt and fresh coriander, to finish

To serve

  • 2 unsliced white loaves (one half-loaf per person)
The story behind the dish

Culture on a plate

Bunny chow was born in Durban, home to one of the largest Indian communities outside India. Many families arrived in the 19th century as indentured workers on the sugar plantations. In the 1940s, eateries began serving curry inside a hollowed loaf of bread — an easy, cheap meal that workers could carry and eat without plates or cutlery.

The name has nothing to do with rabbits: it is often linked to bania, a word for the Indian merchant community who ran many of the shops. Under apartheid, when many people were not allowed to sit in certain restaurants, the bunny was also a meal that could be passed out of a back door. Today it is a proud symbol of Durban and of South Africa's fusion food culture.

Method

Cooking instructions

Toast the whole spices

Heat the oil in a heavy pot. Add the cinnamon, cardamom and curry leaves and fry for 30 seconds until fragrant.

Cook the aromatics

Add the onions and cook for 6–8 minutes until golden. Stir in the garlic, ginger, masala and turmeric and cook for 1 minute — add a splash of water if the spices stick.

Brown the meat

Add the meat and stir until coated in the spices and lightly browned, about 5 minutes.

Simmer the curry

Add the tomatoes and water, cover and simmer for 35–40 minutes. Add the potatoes and cook for 15–20 minutes more, until the meat is tender and the gravy is thick. Stir in the garam masala and salt.

Fill the bread

Cut each loaf in half. Scoop out the soft middle of each half, leaving a thick wall. Spoon the hot curry inside, place the scooped-out bread on top as a “lid” and garnish with coriander.

Cook's tip: Eat a bunny with your hands, starting with the bread “lid” to mop up the gravy. Serve with a carrot and chilli salad (sambals) to cool the heat.
