Ibishyimbo

Red beans slowly simmered and finished with onion, tomato and garlic — Rwanda's everyday comfort food.

  • Cook1 hr 45
  • PrepOvernight soak
  • Serves4
  • LevelEasy
Start cooking
What you need

Ingredients

Change the servings and every quantity updates automatically. Original recipe serves 4.

  • 2 cups dried red kidney beans, soaked overnight
  • 6 cups water
  • 2 tbsp vegetable or palm oil
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 green pepper, diced
  • 1 carrot, diced
  • 1 stock cube
  • Salt, to taste
The story behind the dish

Culture on a plate

In Rwanda, beans (ibishyimbo) are eaten almost every day and are one of the most important sources of protein in the country. Rwandans are among the biggest bean eaters in the world, and farmers grow many varieties on the green hills.

Beans are traditionally cooked in a clay pot over a wood fire, slowly, until soft and creamy. They are served with rice, ubugali (stiff porridge), plantains, sweet potatoes or chapati, and are a centre of family meals shared at the end of the day.

Discover more dishes from this region on our East Africa page.

Method

Cooking instructions

Soak the beans

Cover the dried beans with plenty of water and soak overnight. This shortens the cooking time and makes them easier to digest.

Boil until tender

Drain, add the 6 cups of fresh water and boil for 1 to 1½ hours (traditionally in a clay pot), until the beans are soft. Add more water if needed.

Fry the flavour base

In a separate pan, heat the oil and fry the onion for 5 minutes. Add the garlic, tomatoes, green pepper and carrot and cook until soft.

Combine and simmer

Stir the vegetables into the beans with the crumbled stock cube. Simmer for 15 minutes until thick and creamy, then season with salt.

Serve

Serve hot with rice, ubugali, plantains, sweet potatoes or chapati.

Cook's tip: Add salt only at the end — salting beans too early can keep their skins tough.
