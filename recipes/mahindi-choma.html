Mahindi Choma

Maize roasted over glowing charcoal and rubbed with lime, chilli and salt, East Africa's favourite street snack.

  • Cook25 min
  • Prep10 min
  • Serves4
  • LevelEasy
Start cooking
What you need

Ingredients

Change the servings and every quantity updates automatically. Original recipe serves 4.

  • 4 maize cobs, husks and silk removed
  • 1 lime, cut into wedges
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp butter, melted (optional)
  • Chopped fresh coriander, to finish (optional)
The story behind the dish

Culture on a plate

Mahindi choma means “roasted maize” in Swahili. Across Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda and Rwanda (where it is called ibigori byokeje), vendors line busy roads in the evening, turning cobs over small charcoal stoves until the kernels are smoky and blistered.

The finishing touch is a wedge of lime dipped in chilli and salt and rubbed all over the hot cob. It is cheap, filling and best eaten standing by the roadside with friends, a true taste of everyday East African life.

Method

Cooking instructions

Light the charcoal

Light a charcoal grill or jiko and wait until the coals are glowing red with no big flames, about 15 minutes.

Roast the maize

Place the cobs directly over the coals. Turn every 2–3 minutes for 15–20 minutes, until the kernels are cooked and charred in spots all around.

Mix the chilli salt

Mix the chilli powder and salt on a small plate.

Rub with lime

Dip a lime wedge into the chilli salt and rub it firmly all over each hot cob. Brush with melted butter and sprinkle coriander if you like.

Cook's tip: No charcoal grill? Roast the cobs directly over a gas flame or under a hot oven grill, turning often.
