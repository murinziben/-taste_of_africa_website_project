Injera & Misir Wot

A tangy, spongy teff flatbread served with a slow-cooked red lentil stew spiced with berbere.

  • Cook1 hr 20
  • Prep2–3 days fermenting
  • Serves4
  • LevelMedium
Start cooking
What you need

Ingredients

Change the servings and every quantity updates automatically. Original recipe serves 4.

For the injera

  • 2 cups teff flour
  • 3 cups lukewarm water
  • ¼ tsp salt

For the misir wot

  • 1 cup red lentils, rinsed
  • 2 red onions, very finely chopped
  • 3 tbsp niter kibbeh (spiced butter) or oil
  • 2 tbsp berbere spice blend
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3 cups water
  • Salt, to taste
The story behind the dish

Culture on a plate

Teff — one of the world's smallest grains — has been grown in the Ethiopian and Eritrean highlands for thousands of years. Mixed with water and left to ferment for several days, it becomes the batter for injera, a large, soft flatbread with a sour taste and a surface full of tiny “eyes”.

Meals are shared from one large platter. Diners tear off pieces of injera with the right hand to scoop up stews, and offering a friend a hand-fed bite, called gursha, is a gesture of love and respect. Misir wot, a red lentil stew spiced with berbere, is a favourite on the many fasting days of the Ethiopian Orthodox calendar, when meals are completely plant-based.

Method

Cooking instructions

Start the fermentation

Whisk the teff flour with the lukewarm water until smooth. Cover loosely with a clean cloth and leave at room temperature for 2–3 days. The batter is ready when it smells pleasantly sour and has bubbles on top.

Prepare the batter

Pour off any dark liquid that has collected on top. Stir in the salt and enough water to make a thin batter, like pouring cream.

Cook the injera

Heat a large non-stick pan over medium heat (no oil). Pour the batter in a spiral from the outside to the centre. When holes appear across the surface, cover with a lid for 1 minute. Cook on one side only, then slide onto a cloth to cool.

Soften the onions

For the wot, cook the onions in a dry pot over medium-low heat for 8–10 minutes, stirring, until soft. This traditional dry-cooking step builds a sweet base.

Bloom the spices

Add the niter kibbeh, berbere, garlic, ginger and tomato paste. Stir for 2–3 minutes until fragrant and deep red.

Simmer the lentils

Add the lentils and water. Simmer gently for 25–30 minutes, stirring now and then, until the lentils break down into a thick stew. Season with salt and serve on top of the injera.

Cook's tip: Pure teff injera is naturally gluten-free. If your batter is slow to ferment, a spoonful of batter from a previous batch acts as a starter.
