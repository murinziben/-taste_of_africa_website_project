Jollof Rice

One pot, a deep tomato-and-pepper base and long, gentle steaming — the celebration rice of West Africa.

  • Cook55 min
  • Prep15 min
  • Serves4
  • LevelMedium
Start cooking
What you need

Ingredients

Change the servings and every quantity updates automatically. Original recipe serves 4.

  • 2 cups long-grain parboiled rice, rinsed
  • 4 plum tomatoes, roughly chopped
  • 2 red bell peppers, deseeded
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper (use less for mild)
  • 2 medium onions — 1 blended, 1 sliced
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • ⅓ cup vegetable oil
  • 2½ cups chicken or vegetable stock
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 stock cube, crumbled
  • Salt, to taste
The story behind the dish

Culture on a plate

Jollof rice is the dish no West African party is complete without. Its roots are usually traced to the Wolof (Jolof) Empire of the Senegambia region, where thiéboudienne — rice cooked in a rich tomato and fish stock — was born. As rice cooking spread along the coast, every country made the dish its own.

Today Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Sierra Leone and Liberia all claim the best version, and the playful “jollof wars” are a beloved part of online culture. The Nigerian style below uses long-grain parboiled rice, thyme, curry powder and bay leaves, and is cooked slowly until each grain absorbs the stew.

Method

Cooking instructions

Blend the pepper base

Blend the tomatoes, red bell peppers, scotch bonnet and one onion until smooth. Pour into a pot and boil for 10 minutes to reduce some of the water.

Fry the tomato paste

Heat the oil in a heavy pot. Fry the sliced onion for 2 minutes, then add the tomato paste and fry for 3–4 minutes, stirring, until it darkens slightly. This removes the sour taste.

Cook down the stew

Add the reduced pepper blend, curry powder, thyme, bay leaves and stock cube. Simmer for 15–20 minutes, stirring often, until the oil rises to the top — this is how you know the base is ready.

Add rice and stock

Stir in the rinsed rice so every grain is coated. Pour in the stock, season with salt, and bring to a gentle boil.

Steam low and slow

Cover the pot with foil and then the lid to trap the steam. Cook on the lowest heat for 25–30 minutes without stirring. If the rice is still hard, sprinkle in a little water and cover again.

Toast the bottom (optional)

For “party jollof”, raise the heat for the final 3 minutes to lightly toast the bottom layer — this gives the famous smoky flavour. Rest for 5 minutes, fluff with a fork and serve.

Cook's tip: Parboiled rice is forgiving and stays separate. Avoid stirring once the lid is on — stirring makes jollof mushy.
